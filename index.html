01 · Standardized IoT access solution for motor control systems

This project aimed to make laboratory motor-control equipment easier to debug and connect. I worked as the main embedded and IoT developer, covering cloud configuration, mobile app access, Bluetooth/Wi-Fi module adaptation and STM32-side communication.

  • Created Tuya WBR3 cloud PID and DP function models, then connected the App–cloud–module–MCU communication chain.
  • Iterated the solution into a BT7L-IPEX Bluetooth approach for industrial scenes without router access.
  • Used an oscilloscope to identify USB-to-TTL power ripple causing Bluetooth disconnection and serial checksum errors; reduced 3.3 V ripple from about 150 mV to within 30 mV.
  • Refactored Tuya MCU SDK with HAL Adapter, tuya_port.c, tuya_app.c, UART_IDLE interrupts, ring buffers and an event-driven DP model.
  • Produced a reusable Keil template and README so a new device can be connected in about one hour.

02 · 2025 Smart Car Competition: micro-photoelectric + speed-photoelectric

For the 2025 Chongqing University smart car competition selection, I worked on two groups: micro-photoelectric and speed-photoelectric. The two groups used different chip ecosystems and different debugging methods.

Micro-photoelectric group

Micro-photoelectric group: Infineon TRAVEO platform. My work focused on ESC main-control/current boards, vehicle-model design, mainboard work, and the phototube module.

Speed-photoelectric group · NXP MicroPython track

Speed-photoelectric group: NXP RT1021 MicroPython platform. My work focused on MicroPython control logic, track testing, vehicle-state handling, and speed/steering response.

  • Completed ESC-related PCB work, including the ESC main-control board and ESC current board, plus vehicle-model design for the two cars.
  • Designed, produced and soldered the micro-photoelectric phototube module, then supported hardware debugging so the vehicle could move from PCB layout to track testing.
  • Independently wrote the MicroPython program for the speed-photoelectric group on the NXP RT1021 platform, focusing on line/element response, vehicle-state handling and speed/steering control logic.
  • The team won first prize in the school competition and was selected for the western regional competition.

Course Reflection

Career direction and co-op learning

Career planning, self-assessment, co-op reflection and engineering ethics helped me connect coursework with my next internship choices.

Good engineering is not only making a system move; it is making the system stable, explainable and useful for the people who operate it.

— My co-op reflection
Career path planning

My current direction is embedded systems and industrial automation. The Siemens co-op showed me that the most valuable engineers can bridge software, hardware, mechanics and production communication.

Teamwork and diversity

In both smart-car and lab projects, progress depended on clear task division, frequent integration and respectful communication when different ideas appeared.

Ethical and professional issues

Automation projects affect safety and production quality. I should not hide unstable assumptions; I should document risks, test evidence and operating limits before handover.

Engineers and society

Reliable automation can reduce repetitive work, prevent human error and improve product consistency. The social value comes when the technology is safe, maintainable and understandable.

Future courses and co-op opportunities

I plan to strengthen motor control, embedded systems, robotics, signal processing and industrial communication, and seek co-op roles where I can build deployable automation or IoT systems.
